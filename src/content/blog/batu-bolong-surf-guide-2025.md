Batu Bolong is one of those rare surf breaks that manages to be both beginner-friendly and genuinely satisfying for intermediate surfers. A mellow right-hander that peels over a sandy-bottom reef, it sits at the very bottom of Jalan Pantai Batu Bolong — which means if you're staying at Casa Roca, your board is in the water within three minutes of leaving your room.

Here's what you actually need to know.

## Understanding the Break

Batu Bolong breaks as a right-hander (facing the shore) over a mixed sand-and-reef bottom. The wave is long, forgiving, and rarely closes out all at once — which is why it's been a teaching ground for surf schools and a favourite for longboarders for over a decade.

**Wave type:** Right-hander, beach break with reef sections
**Best for:** Beginners to intermediate, longboards, mini-mals
**Consistency:** Very consistent April–October; smaller and mushier November–March
**Crowd level:** Busy by 08:00, manageable before 07:00 and after 16:00

## Reading the Tides

This is where most visitors go wrong. Batu Bolong is very tide-dependent.

**Best conditions: Mid to high tide**
At low tide, the reef becomes exposed and the wave becomes shallower and more unpredictable. Mid-tide (roughly two to three hours either side of high tide) produces the longest, most consistent rides with the safest ocean floor distance.

**Check tide charts:** The most reliable free tool is [tideschart.com](https://www.tideschart.com) — search "Batu Bolong, Canggu." The Balinese tide cycle means you usually get two highs and two lows per day, roughly six hours apart.

**The golden windows most days:**
- 06:00–08:30 — first light session, clean surface, offshore wind
- 16:30–18:00 — afternoon tide push, golden hour light, less crowded

## Wind & Swell Direction

**Offshore wind:** Southwest to southeast (blows away from shore, cleans up the wave face). This typically occurs in the morning during dry season (April–October).

**Onshore wind:** Picks up around 11:00–13:00 most days, making conditions messier. This is when most experienced surfers get out and get breakfast.

**Swell direction:** South to southwest swell (standard Indian Ocean swell) works best here. A ground swell of 1.0–2.0m is ideal. Above 2.5m it gets powerful and more selective about who should be out there.

## Local Lineup Etiquette

Batu Bolong is a democratic wave — but it has an unwritten social contract. Follow these and you'll be welcomed back.

**1. Don't paddle straight to the peak**
Paddle around the break, not through it. Paddling through an active lineup is dangerous and disrespectful.

**2. The person deepest (furthest out) has right of way**
Don't drop in. Ever. It's the cardinal rule of surfing worldwide, and Batu Bolong has enough eyes to notice.

**3. Greet the locals**
A nod and a smile goes a long way. Many of the regular surfers at Batu Bolong are Balinese locals who grew up on this wave. Respect their priority and their home.

**4. Know your level**
If you're a beginner, stay toward the shoulder of the wave (the less critical section), not at the peak where more powerful waves break. The surf schools operate in specific zones — stay clear unless you're with one.

**5. Don't surf directly in front of the surf school zone**
There's an informal zone near the south end of the beach where surf lessons happen. Give the beginners space — they need more room and can't control their boards as well.

## Renting Boards Near Casa Roca

You don't need to bring a board from home. There are five or six board rental shops along Jl. Pantai Batu Bolong within a three-minute walk of Casa Roca.

**Typical rental rates (2025):**
- Softboard (foamie): IDR 50,000–70,000 per hour
- Longboard: IDR 75,000–100,000 per hour
- Mini-mal: IDR 60,000–80,000 per hour

Full-day rentals are usually 2–3x the hourly rate. Ask to inspect for dings and fin condition before you take it.

## Surf Lessons

For first-timers, the surf schools directly on Batu Bolong beach are professional and safety-conscious. Expect:

- **Group lesson (2 hours):** IDR 350,000–450,000 including board and instructor
- **Private lesson:** IDR 500,000–700,000
- All schools provide rash guards and the first part of the lesson is on the beach

## What to Do When It's Flat

Batu Bolong goes flat a few days per year, usually during swell gaps in the wet season (December–February). On those days:

- **Echo Beach** (1.5km north) picks up more swell and breaks in different conditions
- **Berawa** is more consistent in smaller swells and suits shortboarders better
- **Pererenan** is the least crowded option and often has waves when Batu Bolong is completely flat
